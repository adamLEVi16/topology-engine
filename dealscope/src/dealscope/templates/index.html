What you get

  • A plain-language summary of what the business appears to be and how it appears to make money.
  • Revenue-model inference — SaaS, e-commerce, services, marketplace, media, or hardware — with the signals behind the call.
  • Scale and momentum signals: people named, customers named, hiring activity, publishing cadence, site freshness.
  • Technology and platform dependencies that turn into migration work after a sale.
  • Risk flags and, more usefully, an explicit list of what a website cannot tell you.
  • Questions to ask the seller, tailored to the revenue model detected.
  • Evidence for every claim, each with the URL it came from.

How it reads a site

It fetches the homepage, works out which pages matter — pricing, about, team, careers, customers, legal — and reads those. It identifies itself in its User-Agent, honours robots.txt, waits between requests, and caches what it reads so a repeat run costs the site nothing.

It reads server-rendered HTML only. Sites that build their navigation in the browser will yield less, and the brief says so when that happens rather than reporting an absence as a finding.
